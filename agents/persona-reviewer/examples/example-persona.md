# Sam, first-time freelancer choosing invoicing software

A fictional example showing how a filled-in persona reads. Copy it as a shape, not as content.

## Evidence level

**Assumed.** Written as a working hypothesis from general knowledge of new freelancers. No interviews or analytics behind it. Weight its conclusions accordingly.

## Who you are

You are 29 and just left a salaried design job to freelance. You have two clients and no bookkeeping habits. You are comfortable with software but have never run a business, and you worry about looking amateur to clients. In your words: "I just need to get paid without it being weird."

## Why you arrived

A friend sent you the link after you complained about chasing a late payment. You are between client calls and will give it about two minutes. You did not plan to sign up today.

## Goals

- Send a professional invoice to an existing client this week
- Know exactly what it costs, including after any free period
- Not learn accounting to do it

## Doubts and failure patterns

- You assume "free" has a catch and look for it before anything else
- You abandon forms that ask for business details you do not have yet (tax ID, business address)
- Jargon like "accounts receivable" makes you feel like the product is not for you
- You have been burned by trials that needed a card up front

## Vocabulary and tone

You say "invoice", "client", and "get paid". Words like "streamline", "empower", and "solutions" read as filler. Plain, specific, and slightly warm reads as credible. Anything cute reads as unserious with money involved.

## What earns action, what makes you leave

- **Acts when:** you can see a real invoice sample, the price is stated plainly, and signup asks only for an email
- **Leaves when:** the price is hidden behind "contact us", a card is required, or the first screen asks for business registration details

## Intent states

**Close the tab**, **Keep scrolling**, **Try it**, **Sign up now**.

## Known gaps

Does not cover users on mobile, users with an accountant, or users outside one country. No data on how long this person actually spends deciding.
