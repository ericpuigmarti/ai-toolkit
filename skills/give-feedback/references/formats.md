# Feedback Formats — Full Templates

Read this after Steps 1–3 in SKILL.md have identified direction, purpose, and the facts.
Pick the matching template below, then draft.

---

## By direction

### 1. Peer feedback — ALIGN

Use when colleagues share responsibility but neither manages the other.

- **Aim**: "I want us to make the next handoff smoother."
- **Locate**: "In Tuesday's launch review…"
- **Identify**: "The revised requirements arrived after implementation began."
- **Name impact**: "That created rework and put the deadline at risk."
- **Generate together**: "What was happening on your side, and how should we handle changes next time?"

**Template:**
> I'd like to discuss [shared goal]. During [specific situation], I observed [behavior/work]. The
> impact was [effect]. How did you see it? Could we try [possible next step]?

---

### 2. Upward feedback — PERSPECTIVE–IMPACT–REQUEST

Use when giving feedback to a manager or senior leader.

**Template:**
> Would you be open to feedback about [topic]? I may not have all the context. When [specific
> event] happened, I observed [behavior/decision]. The effect on me/the team was [impact]. How did
> you see it? In the future, would you consider [specific request]?

**Rules:**
- Make the request concrete and feasible
- Distinguish personal impact from assumed team-wide sentiment
- Say "I noticed" or "I experienced," unless broader evidence exists
- Avoid diagnosing the manager's motives
- Account for psychological safety and possible retaliation risk
- When appropriate, ask the manager for feedback first — Radical Candor explicitly recommends
  soliciting guidance before giving it

---

### 3. Downward coaching — EXPECTATION–GAP–SUPPORT

Use when a manager gives developmental feedback to a direct report.

**Template:**
> I want to help you succeed with [responsibility or goal]. We agreed that [expectation]. In
> [specific situation], I observed [behavior/work], compared with the expectation of [gap]. This
> resulted in [impact]. What's your perspective? Going forward, I need [required change]. I can
> support you by [support], and we'll check progress on [date/event].

This format prevents managers from treating an unstated expectation as an employee failure.

## By purpose

### Reinforcing feedback

Use to make effective behavior repeatable — not merely to compliment someone.

**Template:**
> When [context], you [specific action]. That resulted in [valuable impact]. Please keep doing
> [repeatable behavior], especially when [future situation].

**Bad:** "Great presentation."

**Better:**
> In the roadmap review, you stated the recommendation before walking through the evidence. That
> helped the group understand the decision and resolve objections quickly. Keep using that
> approach in executive reviews.

---

### Course-correcting feedback

Use for a contained behavior that can be changed quickly.

**Template:**
> When [context], I observed [behavior]. It resulted in [impact]. I may be missing context — what
> happened from your perspective? Next time, please [specific alternative].

---

### Developmental feedback

Use for a recurring skill or behavior rather than a single incident.

**Template:**
> I've noticed a pattern across [examples]: [behavior]. This matters because [impact on role or
> goals]. A stronger version would look like [observable target behavior]. What would help you
> practice that? Let's try [action] and revisit it [time].

---

### Serious performance feedback

Use when the pattern affects role expectations and requires explicit accountability.

**Template:**
> The expectation is [standard]. On [examples], the outcome was [evidence]. The impact is
> [business/team impact]. The required change is [measurable result] by [time]. I will provide
> [support]. We'll review progress on [date]. If the expectation is not met, [accurate, authorized
> consequence].

**Never invent HR consequences or present coaching as a formal warning unless the user confirms
that status.**

---

### Feedforward

Use when evidence is incomplete or the user wants a lower-friction developmental conversation.

**Template:**
> For the next [event], one change that could improve [outcome] is [behavior], because
> [anticipated impact]. How does that suggestion fit with your perspective?
