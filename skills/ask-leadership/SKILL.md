# AAA: Altitude, Audience, Action

A framework for structuring communication with execs and senior leaders — learned from an internally-famous workshop run by an engineering director at Google in New York. Useful before any exec update, but also for status reports, pitches, sign-off asks, or design reviews with leadership.

## The three "aha" moments (worth surfacing to Eric if he's new to this or teaching it to someone else)

1. **They're not thinking about your thing.** An IC spends most of their mindshare on one problem. The exec spends a sliver of theirs — this is one of 20 topics they're dropped into today, between hundreds of Slack messages.
2. **Execs don't hate detail — they want the *right* detail.** You and they operate at different altitudes; both matter. Your job is to meet them at theirs and bring them into exactly the evidence they need — not more, not less.
3. **Structure usually needs to invert.** Most people build up: context → what I did → more detail → conclusion. Execs are filtering for "is this important, and what do I need to do with it?" — so that goes first, not last.

## How to use this with Eric

The three As aren't strictly sequential — expect to go back and forth. But **Altitude depends on nailing Audience and Action first**, so gather those before locking the framing. Ask Eric (adapt based on what he's already told you in the conversation — don't re-ask what you already know):

**Audience**
- Who specifically is this for, and which of their priorities/metrics does this connect to?
- How much context do they already have on this area? What's the smallest amount they'd need to get it fast?
- Do they have known pushbacks, pet topics, or things they always ask about?
- What do they need to make a fast decision — how much data, whose point of view do they trust?
- What's their meeting style (slides vs. no slides, pre-read expected, etc.)?

**Action**
- What does Eric need to be true when this meeting/message ends?
- What specific action does he need from this person — a decision, a resource, a sign-off, or just informed alignment? (Even for alignment, they'll want to know "what do you need from me?")

**Altitude**
- What's the single most important thing to land, said in 30 seconds?
- If they respond "so what, what does this mean?" — what's the answer?
- What are the 2-3 strongest points, each with its best piece of evidence?
- What's the detail that can live one layer down (an appendix, a linked doc) rather than up top?
- If the meeting got cut to 5 minutes, what's the one thing that has to survive?

## Producing the output

Once you have enough on Audience and Action, draft the update inverted — headline/ask first, then the 2-3 supporting points with evidence, then detail available on request. Keep it short enough to say out loud in under a minute before anyone asks a follow-up. Flag anything you're inferring rather than something Eric told you, so he can correct it.

Don't force all of this into a rigid template — the point is the thinking, not the format. A short Slack update and a board-level pitch both use AAA, but look nothing alike.

## One caveat worth keeping in the output when relevant

Altitude isn't "stay high-level the whole time." Good leaders can and do drop into execution detail; that's part of why they're effective. Eric often knows more than the exec about the specific problem — AAA is about meeting them where they are, not deferring to them.
